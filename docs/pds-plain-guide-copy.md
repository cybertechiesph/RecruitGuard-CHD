## Page title

How to fill up your Personal Data Sheet (PDS)

## Intro

The Personal Data Sheet (CS Form No. 212) is the standard government form that
tells us who you are, where you studied, and what work you have done. Every
applicant submits one. This page explains it in plain language and points out
the mistakes we see most often. It follows the Civil Service Commission's
official guide — if anything here is ever unclear, the CSC guide is the final
word.

**Download first:**
- Blank PDS form (Excel, CS Form No. 212 – Revised 2026)
- Official CSC guide (PDF)

## Before you start

You will need:
- A computer with Excel or a free alternative (Google Sheets, LibreOffice).
  **The PDS must be typed, not handwritten.**
- Your school records, eligibility or licence details, and job history —
  including exact dates.
- A recent passport-size photo (4.5 cm × 3.5 cm), taken within the last six
  months, no filters.
- Any valid government ID.

Take your time. It is normal for this to take an hour or two the first time.
Save your file as you go.

## The five rules that cause the most corrections

1. **Write your name in this order: SURNAME, FIRST NAME, NAME EXTENSION,
   MIDDLE NAME.** Name extension means Jr., Sr., III — leave it blank if you
   have none. Use the same order for your spouse and your parents.
2. **Dates are day/month/year (dd/mm/yyyy).** 5 March 2001 is `05/03/2001`,
   not `03/05/2001`.
3. **Never leave a box empty. Write `N/A` if it does not apply to you.** A
   blank box looks like a forgotten answer; `N/A` shows you read the question.
4. **Do not abbreviate.** Write the complete name of every school, office, and
   organisation — *Civil Service Commission*, not *CSC*; *BS Psychology*, not
   *BSP*.
5. **Answer everything truthfully.** The form carries a legal warning: a false
   statement in the PDS can be grounds for an administrative or criminal case.

## Section by section

### I. Personal information
Your full name, birth date and birthplace, sex, civil status, height, weight,
citizenship, addresses, and contact details. If your civil status is "Others",
say what it is. If you hold dual or foreign citizenship, pick the country from
the list in the form. "Agency employee number" means your employee ID in the
office where you work now — write `N/A` if you are not a government employee.

### II. Family background
Your spouse, your parents, and **all** your children with their birth dates.
Two things people miss:
- Your mother's name here is her **maiden name** — her name before she married
  (*pangalan bago ikasal*).
- List every child, even those who are adults or no longer living with you.

### III. Educational background
For each level — elementary, secondary, vocational, college, graduate studies:
- The **full** name of the school.
- The course, written in full.
- The school years you attended, like `2015-2019`.
- If you finished, the year you graduated. **If you did not finish, write the
  highest grade, level, or number of units you earned** — that is a valid
  answer, not a blank.
- Any scholarship or academic honour you received.

For elementary, write *PRIMARY EDUCATION* if you graduated. For high school,
write *HIGH SCHOOL* if you finished under the old curriculum, or *JUNIOR HIGH
SCHOOL* / *SENIOR HIGH SCHOOL* under K-12.

### IV. Civil service eligibility
Every eligibility you hold, with your rating, and the date and place of the
examination or conferment. This includes eligibilities granted by law, not only
exams — honour graduate, barangay official, veteran preference, and similar.
If your eligibility is a professional licence (RA 1080), add the licence number
and its expiry date.

Write `N/A` if you have none. Not having an eligibility does not disqualify you
from every position.

### V. Work experience
All your jobs — government and private — **starting with your current or most
recent one**. For each: exact start and end dates, the complete position title,
the complete name of the office or company, your monthly salary in figures
(for example `P21,877.00`), salary grade and step increment if you had one
(written like `24-2`), your employment status (permanent, temporary, casual,
contractual), and whether it was government service (yes or no).

**Important for job applicants:** the CSC also asks for a separate **Work
Experience Sheet (WES)** when you apply for a vacant position. It lists only
the experience relevant to the job you are applying for, and it is submitted
together with your PDS.

### VI. Voluntary work
Any volunteer work with an organisation — the organisation's complete name and
address, your dates, the number of hours, and what you did there. Barangay,
church, school, and community volunteer work all count. Write `N/A` if you have
none.

### VII. Learning and development (trainings and seminars)
Trainings, seminars, and workshops you attended, **most recent first**: the
full title of the program, the dates, the number of hours, the type
(managerial, supervisory, technical), and the complete name of the office or
institution that ran it.

### VIII. Other information
Your special skills and hobbies; any non-academic awards, recognitions or
distinctions, written out in full; and any professional association you belong
to, also written in full.

### Questions 34 to 40
These are the sworn questions — about relationships to people in the agency,
administrative or criminal cases, convictions, separation from service,
election candidacy, and indigenous or PWD status. Answer every one, and
**whenever you answer "Yes", give the details** in the space provided.

### Question 41 — references
Three people who can vouch for you, and who are **not related to you**. Give
each one's complete name, their office or home address, and a contact number
or email. Tell them you listed them.

### Question 42 — the last step
- Sign the form (or use your right thumbmark if you cannot sign).
- Write your government ID number and the date it was issued.
- Attach your passport-size photo.
- Put your signature and the date on the bottom of every page.

## Before you upload it to this portal

1. **Print only the extra sheets you actually used.** The workbook has
   continuation sheets (C5 to C11) for people who need more rows. If you did
   not use one, do not include it.
2. **Save your accomplished PDS as a PDF** — this portal accepts PDF, JPG, and
   PNG files, not Excel files. In Excel: *File → Save as* (or *Export*) and
   choose PDF. If you printed and signed a paper copy, a clear scan or photo of
   every page is fine.
3. Check that your name, dates, and school names read exactly as they do on
   your other documents. Mismatches are the most common reason we return an
   application for correction.
4. Upload it on the application form under **Personal Data Sheet**.

If you are returned a document for correction, you will get an email with what
to fix and a deadline. You can re-upload from the same link — you do not start
over.
